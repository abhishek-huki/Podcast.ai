# Quality rubric and the V1 → V2 → V3 loop

Self-scoring is not a grade to defend. It is the mechanism that finds the next gap.
A brief that scores itself 9/10 on the first pass has stopped improving; the honest
6/10 with a specific gap list is the one that ends up good.

## Why iterate at all

V1 collects whatever the tools return easily. It always has holes: a platform that
returned four items, transcripts skimmed instead of analyzed, comments not touched.
V2 fills the named holes. V3 adds the expensive sources that were deferred. In the
original build, V1 was interesting, V2 scored ~7/10, and only V3 was usable — and the
difference was entirely in research depth, not in question-writing effort.

So: never present V1 as the deliverable, and never move to question writing while
guest research scores below 8.

## Research depth (score /10)

| Points | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 2 | Every Tier 1 source attempted, with volumes reported (not "checked X" but "pulled 34 posts") |
| 1 | At least 3 full transcripts analyzed, not just skimmed |
| 1 | Recent delta covers the last 30 days with dated evidence |
| 1 | Kill list has 10+ entries with evidence links |
| 1 | 3+ unfinished threads with exact quotes and timestamps |
| 1 | Evolution timeline with dated positions |
| 1 | Independent topic research done, not just guest-anchored |
| 1 | Content-supply gaps named specifically, with demand evidence |
| 1 | Every failed source explicitly declared |

Thin-pull test: if any platform returned fewer than ~10 items, that's a hole, and the
score cannot exceed 6 until it's retried or explicitly declared unrecoverable.

## Question quality (score /10)

| Points | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 2 | Every question traces to specific research evidence, cited |
| 2 | Nothing on the kill list appears |
| 1 | Arc runs beginner → advanced without a difficulty cliff |
| 1 | Each question has a predicted answer and a follow-up |
| 1 | At least 5 questions could only be asked of this guest |
| 1 | Movement 6 gives a genuinely implementable framework |
| 1 | Differentiated from the guest's biggest existing appearance |
| 1 | Timings sum to the target episode length |

## Deliverable integrity (score /10)

| Points | Criterion |
|---|---|
| 3 | Every factual claim tagged V/P/I/U with sources |
| 2 | Names, titles, companies, dates verified |
| 2 | Runsheet fits one page and is scannable |
| 1 | Landmines section present and specific |
| 1 | Raw data saved and re-analyzable |
| 1 | Actual scraping cost reported |

## The critique step

After each version, write the critique before writing the next version. Answer, in
writing and specifically:

1. What worked, with volumes?
2. What is thin or missing, named source by source?
3. Which sources failed, and what was tried?
4. What did it cost?
5. Is this deep enough to sustain a 2-hour masterclass — and if not, what exactly is
   missing?
6. What would move the score up 2 points?

Then do #6. "Go deeper" as an instruction to yourself is useless; "pull 6 more
appearances, analyze full transcripts of the top 3, and compare against the two
existing long-form interviews" is a plan.

## Stopping

Stop when research ≥8, questions ≥8, integrity ≥9, or the user says it's enough.
Report the final scores plainly along with what a higher score would have required —
the host deserves to know where the brief is weak before they walk into the room.
