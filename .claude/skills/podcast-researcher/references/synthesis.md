# Synthesis: finding the Gold Zone

Two research streams now exist — what the guest uniquely knows, and what the market
is missing. This stage collides them and picks the episode.

## The two axes

**Guest uniqueness (1–5).** Could another credible person explain this just as well?
5 = almost nobody else has done this or has this data. 1 = any competent commentator
could cover it.

Evidence for a high score: they built it, they ran it at scale, they have the numbers,
they were there, they changed their mind publicly and can explain why. Evidence for a
low score: they are summarizing other people's work, however well.

**Content-supply gap (1–5).** How badly does good material on this not exist?
5 = high demand, nothing specific and credible available. 1 = saturated.

## The 2×2

```
                 high supply gap
                       ▲
   NICHE BUT NEW       │      GOLD ZONE
   (maybe a segment)   │   (build the episode here)
                       │
◄──────────────────────┼──────────────────────► high guest uniqueness
   COMMODITY           │      REPEAT SHOW
   (skip)              │   (they've said it 40x)
                       ▼
```

- **Gold Zone** — unique + unmet. The spine of the episode.
- **Repeat show** — the guest is great at it, but it's everywhere already. At most a
  fast recap so newcomers aren't lost; never a segment.
- **Niche but new** — real gap, but the guest isn't the right person. One question.
- **Commodity** — cut entirely.

Score 10–15 candidate subtopics drawn from the guest brief (themes, unfinished
threads, recent delta) and the topic brief (gaps, contested beliefs). Put the scores
in a table with a one-line justification each — visible reasoning makes it obvious
when a favourite topic is actually a repeat.

## From Gold Zone to episode angles

Turn the top-scoring cluster into 3–5 candidate angles. Each angle gets:

- **Working title** — what the episode is really about
- **The promise** — what the viewer can do after 2 hours that they couldn't before
- **Why this guest** — the uniqueness evidence
- **Why now** — the recent delta or market change that makes it timely
- **Differentiation** — how it avoids the guest's existing interviews, named explicitly
- **Risk** — where it could get thin, and what backup material exists

Present these to the user and let them pick. Do not proceed to questions on an
unchosen angle; the sequencing depends entirely on which promise the episode makes.

## Differentiation check

Before locking the angle, compare against the guest's biggest existing appearance
and against the host's own previous episodes on this topic. Write one explicit
paragraph: *what does this episode do that those did not?* If that paragraph is hard
to write, the angle is wrong — go back to the unfinished threads and the recent delta,
which are the two places genuinely new material hides.

## Output

Write `03-gold-zone.md`: the scoring table, the 2×2 placement, the candidate angles,
the user's chosen angle, and the differentiation paragraph. The chosen angle is the
input to question design; everything else is the audit trail for why.
